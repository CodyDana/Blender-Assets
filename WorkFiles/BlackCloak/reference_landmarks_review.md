## Collar crease families

The collar is a loose stacked scarf. Front crease paths are **asymmetric, sagged curves**, not level lathed rings or one regular helix. These approximate guides describe the visible overlapping lips; they may be partially hidden by the adjacent fold.

- **Opening rim:** ellipse-like rear/top arc `(143,17) → (156,10) → (189,7) → (221,9) → (240,16)`; front rim crosses approximately `(145,22) → (168,29) → (200,31) → (225,27) → (240,20)`.
- **Upper crossed fold:** `(144,25) → (160,35) → (181,43) → (204,45) → (224,38) → (240,28)`. Its front lip is oblique, with a left-to-center downward sweep.
- **Next compressed fold:** approximately `(139,39) → (156,50) → (182,57) → (207,58) → (230,52) → (248,43)`.
- **Middle scarf fold:** approximately `(132,51) → (149,67) → (176,76) → (202,77) → (232,68) → (259,58)`.
- **Lower spread fold:** approximately `(125,66) → (143,84) → (172,96) → (204,99) → (237,89) → (269,68)`.
- **Lowest fold transitions into mantle:** approximately `(121,82) → (139,96) → (172,105) → (204,107) → (238,99) → (276,77)`.

Important shape rules: upper folds remain relatively close to the neck; lower folds flare sideways. Crease depth and spacing change along every curve. There are small tucks on the left, while the right lower wraps stretch into broader diagonal bands. The front of the scarf should not look like an armored tube. Match the whole collar silhouette before tuning each crease.

## Priority for the next render comparison

1. Align camera to a near-frontal view, collar top y7, lowest hem y661, and same overall aspect ratio. Do not compare a strong three-quarter view as though its widths were front-view measurements.
2. Match the four dominant points: clasp `(117,90)`, left wing `(10,332)`, right wing `(402,380)`, long front leaf tip `(374,640)`.
3. Match collar flare and irregular crossing creases; eliminate the regular cylindrical wrapped-band silhouette.
4. Match the two short upper mantle edges and one long sweeping diagonal edge. Keep their shapes different.
5. Match the front leaf's left boundary and diagonal hem, preserving the deep opening immediately to its left.
6. Add only the major broad folds first; remove uniform curtain pleats before introducing smaller wrinkles.
7. Use restrained matte black fabric. A large pebble/vein bump pattern must not replace the finer, low-contrast grain in the reference.

Exact fold correspondence cannot be proven from one photograph because lighting and hidden geometry are ambiguous. The coordinates make the visible shape comparison concrete; they do not justify calling the reconstructed asset exact.
