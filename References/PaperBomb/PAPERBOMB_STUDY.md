# Paper Bomb Asset Study

**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Status:** BUILT, then revised. The asset shipped, was reviewed three ways (geometry, Unreal, visual) and was
rebuilt. Section 13 records every number in this study that the build proved wrong, and section 11's open
questions are answered rather than open. Read section 13 before building the fan or the smoke bomb from this
document — several of the numbers below are superseded there and are left in place so the reasoning survives.

**Purpose.** Build-to numbers, layout, surface and pipeline for a ninja paper bomb (an explosive paper tag in the
ofuda/talisman tradition), the next piece after the shuriken pack. It is the first asset of the new `Scripts/props/`
package, so everything here that is not tag-specific — sheet generator, typesetting, brush strokes, paper material,
atlas helper — is written to be reused by the folding fan and the smoke bomb.

Flags as in `KUNAI_STUDY.md` and `SHURIKEN_STUDY.md`:
**SOURCED** = measured, with a URL or a measured file. **DERIVED** = computed from sourced figures.
**ESTIMATE** = a design choice. **SNIPPET** = seen only in a search summary, the page itself was not readable.
**MEASURED** = measured here, from the guide PNGs or from project files, by the scripts in section 12.

Companions: `sources.md` (the `[n]` markers) and `WorkFiles/paperbomb/study_calc/` — `measure_guide.py`,
`measure_guide2.py`, `measure_guide3.py`, `measure_guide4.py`, `centre.py`, `fontcheck.py`, `fontname.py`,
`renderstats.py` with their `.json` output [55].

Four facts shape the build. **One:** the guide images are a ChatGPT illustration, not an albedo map — their paper
reads at linear luma 0.79 and their ink at 0.005, both outside the physical range, so every colour here is
re-derived, not sampled. **Two:** the guide's proportions are not those of a real ofuda (0.447 against 0.27–0.35),
so the aspect is the guide's and the *size* comes from the hōsho sheet it would be cut from. **Three:** a 156 mm
object on a 2048 map caps at 129 px/cm, which is the whole texture plan. **Four:** paper is 0.15 mm thick, so the
silhouette is curl and tear, not thickness — a flat plane would kill the asset, and so would a 2 mm slab.

## 2. The real object: Japanese paper talismans

The paper bomb has no real-world original. Its *container* does: the Japanese paper talisman, a family of printed
paper slips that is centuries old, entirely generic, and the correct thing to build to.

**Aspect ratios.** Real talismans are narrow: 0.265 to 0.354 (w/h), senjafuda exactly 0.333. Only the envelopes
reach our range (120 × 250 = 0.48). The guide's 0.447 is therefore **wider than any real ofuda** — a deliberate
graphic-novel proportion, not an error to fix. Keep it; it is what the user drew and what reads at a distance.

**The parent sheet.** Senjafuda are cut from 奉書全判, the full hōsho sheet, **393.9 × 530.25 mm** (1 尺 3 寸 ×
1 尺 7 寸 5 分), sixteen-up: nominal 197 × 66.5 mm, trimmed to 174 × 58 mm, i.e. a trim factor of **0.872 across
and 0.883 along** [7]. That sheet, and that trim, give our size (section 4).

**Material.** Ofuda and gofu are printed on 奉書紙 (hōsho), a kozo washi; cheap modern ones use 上質紙 or a white
roll stock [11][13]. Measured kozo washi runs **0.11–0.13 mm thick** in ordinary weights and 0.20 mm in reinforced
grades [14]. Echizen hōsho kozo is **62 g/m²** on a 550 × 440 mm sheet [15]. Commercial kozo sheets run 30 g/m²
[16], 39 g/m² [58], 46, 70 and 110 g/m² [17]; the trade bands are 3.7–9 g/m² tissue, 10–25 light and translucent,
25–45 average, 45–70 opaque with body [18]. Western coated stock is far denser: 上質紙 at 81.5 g/m² is 0.100 mm,
104.7 g/m² is 0.130 mm, 157 g/m² is 0.195 mm [19], and the printer's rule of thumb is thickness ≈ g/m² ÷ 800 [20],
i.e. **0.82 g/cm³**. Kozo at 62 g/m² and 0.115 mm is **0.54 g/cm³** — washi is about two-thirds the bulk density
of machine paper, which is exactly why it is soft, opaque at low weight and tears the way it does.

**How they are printed.** Historically woodblock; ofuda descend from temple woodblock prints of the Nara and Heian
periods [1]. The modern trade prints them on hōsho in **two colours, black and vermilion**, with the shrine's seal
applied as a separate wood or rubber stamp in 朱肉 (oil-based vermilion paste) that has to be cleaned off the stamp
with alcohol because it degrades it [13]. That two-colour black-and-red scheme is the single most useful
authenticity fact in this study: the guide already obeys it, and our tag should carry **no third ink**.

**How they age.** A paper fuda is meant to be replaced every year [10]; it lives on a household shelf or pasted on a
gate [1], gets handled, sooted by incense, sun-bleached at the exposed edge and foxed by damp. Aged paper yellows by
cellulose oxidation, and foxing appears as discrete brown spots [30]. For us: edge darkening, low-frequency
blotching, a few small warm spots, thumb grime at the bottom corners.

**How they curl and tear.** Paper fibres are hygroexpansive — they swell and shrink with relative humidity — and a
*gradient of moisture through the thickness* is what produces curl rather than plain expansion; the sheet bends
toward the side that dries last, and a printed side responds more slowly than the bare side, so print itself biases
the curl [25][26][27]. Washi tears with a soft feathered edge instead of a clean line, because kozo fibres are
10–15 mm long and interlock more like cloth than like paper; handmade sheets carry a fibrous deckle edge for the
same reason [21][22][23][24]. So: a printed tag curls **toward its printed face**, and any tear shows a 0.5–1.5 mm
fibre fringe, never a straight break.

**Two of those are traps.** The paper's linear 0.913 red channel is above the physical ceiling for a dielectric
(60–240 sRGB, i.e. 0.047–0.87 linear [31][32]) and the ink's linear 0.005 is far below the floor. The guide is a
*lit illustration*, not a base-colour map. Section 6 re-derives both.

**What the ring really is.** Not a circle: 485 × 609 px, an ellipse 1.256 taller than wide, centred right of the
card's centre line at x 0.522. Its stroke varies by a factor of five along its length (0.8 to 3.8 mm at our size)
and it fills only **15 %** of its own bounding box. That is a single fast brush lap with the wrist, not a drawn
annulus, and it is the element most likely to be got wrong.

**What the two versions disagree about.** v2's aspect is 0.430 against v1's 0.447 (v2 was re-cropped by hand) and
its ink coverage is 2 points higher (real kanji have more strokes than v1's invented marks). Everything structural
— rule insets, ring, seal boxes, column centres, corner clip — agrees to within 0.005 of the card box. **Build to
v1's proportions and v2's glyph placement.**

**v1's flanking columns and lower text are not writing.** They are invented script-looking marks; only 火道 in the
small seal box is a real pair of characters. v2 replaced them with real kanji. This is exactly the failure mode the
brief forbids: **every mark on our tag is either a real character from the font or a deliberate ornament, never a
fake glyph.**

## 4. Build-to table

**Frame.** The card lies in the XY plane. **+X toward the top of the tag**, +Y to the left of the printed face as
seen from +Z, **+Z out of the printed front face**, thickness along Z. This matches the pack: the shuriken plates
lie in XY with +Z the presented face, the kunai's long axis is +X [54], and the pipeline exports Forward −Y / Up Z
[51][53].

Guide fraction → millimetre mapping, which the art module should carry as one function:

```
X_mm = (0.5 - y_frac) * 156.0        # y_frac 0 = top of the tag
Y_mm = (0.5 - x_frac) *  70.0        # x_frac 0 = left edge of the printed face, seen from +Z
```

### 5.5 Brush character — the part numbers cannot carry

Judge this by looking at the render, not by the table.

* **Weight variation.** Every red and black stroke changes width by at least 2:1 along its length. The ring does
  5:1 (0.8 → 3.8 mm). A constant-width stroke anywhere reads as vector art and fails.
* **Dry brush.** The ring is 97 % angularly present but only 15 % of its box is ink: the stroke is shot through
  with streaks of bare paper running *along* the stroke, widening where the brush lifted. Two or three true gaps of
  2–6° only.
* **Ink bleed.** Heavy strokes wick 0.15–0.4 mm into the fibre with a soft, slightly ragged boundary; the
  bleed is stronger across the fibre than along it. Thin, fast strokes barely bleed at all.
* **Load falloff.** Each column's first character is the wettest and darkest; by the third the ink is drier and
  10–20 % lighter. The centre 爆 is a fully loaded brush — it is the darkest thing on the tag.
* **Nothing geometric.** The rules are ruled but drawn with a brush: ±0.05 mm of wander, a slight swell at each
  start, the corner brackets hand-hooked. The seal boxes are stamped, so their edges are *crisper* than the
  brushwork but broken by ink starvation at 5–15 % of their length.
* **Registration.** A stamped seal is never perfectly square to a printed frame: rotate each seal box by 0.6–1.2°
  and offset it 0.2–0.4 mm from its nominal centre.

## 7. Texture plan

| Map | Size | Colourspace | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| `T_PaperBomb_BC` | 2048 × 2048 | sRGB | Base colour: paper, ink, seals |
| `T_PaperBomb_ORM` | 2048 × 2048 | linear | R = AO, G = roughness, B = metallic (0) |
| `T_PaperBomb_N` | 2048 × 2048 | linear, normal | Tangent-space, **DirectX green** (pack convention) |
| `T_PaperBomb_M` | 2048 × 2048 | linear | R = torn-edge opacity / fibre fringe, G = scorch gradient out from the flame emblem (for the game to animate a burn), B = ink mask (lets the user recolour the print without touching BC) |

**Atlas, and why 2048 is the cap.** The tag is 156 mm on its long side. With 16 px of padding top and bottom, a
2048 map gives 2016 px for 156 mm = **12.92 px/mm = 129 px/cm**. Rotating the island does not help — the long side
is the long side. So **129 px/cm is the hard ceiling on a 2048 map** for this object, a little under the pack's
measured 134–170 px/cm [54]. Rising to 4096 would give 258 px/cm, but `ASSET_GUIDELINES.md` reserves 4K for hero
and skin [51]. **Ship 2048** and note 4096 as a user option.

| Island | Size (px) | U range | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front face | 905 × 2016 | 0.008 → 0.450 | 12.92 px/mm |
| Back face | 905 × 2016 | 0.458 → 0.900 | 12.92 px/mm |
| Rim, 3 stacked vertical strips | 2 × 2016, 2 × 2016, 2 × 1579 (rim develops to 434.2 mm × 0.15 mm) | 0.905 → 1.0 | 12.92 px/mm |

Packing efficiency **87.3 %**. Padding 16 px at 2K, Pack Islands margin 16/2048 = 0.008, Rotation Cardinal [51].
The front island must **not** be rotated or mirrored after the bake, and the two faces get separate islands so the
back can carry its own wear — a paper tag's back is where the thumb grime and the glue ghost live.

**Legibility at the LOD1 switch.** LOD1 takes over at 0.889 m (section 8), where the tag is **75.6 × 168.5 px** at
1080p. The front island is 905 × 2016 texels for that, a ratio of **11.97**, so mip 3–4 is what the GPU samples.
Any feature thinner than about **12 texels = 0.93 mm** washes out at the switch.

| Feature | Size on the tag | Texels | Reads at 0.89 m? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Centre 爆, main strokes | 3–8 mm | 39–103 | Yes, clearly |
| Column characters, em 14.4 mm | strokes 1.0–2.5 mm | 13–32 | Yes as characters |
| Ring stroke | 0.8–3.8 mm | 10–49 | Yes |
| Outer rule at the guide's 0.80 mm | 0.80 mm | 10 | Marginal — **build it at 0.90 mm** |
| 火道, em 7.5 mm | strokes ≈ 0.5 mm | 6 | No — softens to a red tone. Accepted |
| Side lozenge, 0.56 mm | 0.56 mm | 7 | No. Accepted: it is punctuation, not information |

**Design rule for the art agent: anything that must still be legible at the LOD1 switch is drawn at ≥ 1.0 mm
(13 texels).** That is why the rule weight is specified at 0.90 mm rather than the guide's measured 0.80.

## 8. Geometry plan and LODs

**Construction.** A position-keyed vertex factory on a 1 nm grid, as the pack requires — never `bmesh.ops.bevel`
[51][54]. The card is one generator: an octagonal outline (rectangle with four 45° corner clips), a regular grid
across it, then the curl, the two folds and the dog-ear applied as displacement of that grid, then the torn stretch
cut into the outline, then a rim solidified to 0.15 mm. Front and back are separate face sets so they can take
separate UV islands and separate wear.

| LOD | Screen size | Triangles | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| LOD0 | **1.0** | **1,150** (band 1,000–1,600) | Front and back grids 10 × 22 quads (440 tris each), rim 48 quads (96 tris), an extra row at each fold, the torn outline and the dog-ear |
| LOD1 | **0.1710** | **420** (band 350–650) | Grids 6 × 12 (144 tris each), rim 28 quads, folds kept as single creases, torn outline simplified to 4 lobes, dog-ear kept |
| LOD2 | **0.0599** | **130** (band 100–200) | Grids 3 × 6 (36 tris each), rim 14 quads, curl reduced to one bend, torn edge straight, dog-ear dropped |

Budget context: the project prop budget is 1,000–5,000 triangles [51], and the shuriken pack's own bands are
1,200–2,500 / 500–900 / 120–250 [52].

**Screen sizes.** The pack rule is 1.0 / 0.10 / 0.035 scaled by the form's bounds radius over 50 mm
(`spec.scaled_lod_screen_sizes`) [52]. At R = 85.50 mm the factor is 1.710, giving **1.0 / 0.1710 / 0.0599**, so
the switch *distances* stay the pack's:

| | Screen size | Distance (90° hFOV, 16:9) | Tag on screen at 1080p |
|---|---|---|---|
| LOD0 → LOD1 | 0.1710 | **0.889 m** | 75.6 × 168.5 px |
| LOD1 → LOD2 | 0.0599 | **2.538 m** | 26.5 × 59.0 px |

At 59 px tall the 爆 is still 23 px and reads; at 26 px wide the ring is 21 px. LOD2 keeps the silhouette, the
curl and the print, and loses only the fold geometry. An FBX LodGroup carries no thresholds, so these must be
applied after import from the sidecar, exactly as the kunai does [53].

**Collision.** One hull, **`UCX_SM_PaperBomb_LOD0_00`** — the name must repeat the *render mesh node* name
exactly, so `UCX_SM_PaperBomb_00` on a node called `SM_PaperBomb_LOD0` imports with convex count 0 and no
collision at all [33][51]. Take the flat octagonal outline and inflate it to **±1.5 mm in Z** (12–16 vertices): a
0.15 mm hull is degenerate for the solver. If the tag still tunnels when thrown, turn on CCD on the projectile
rather than thickening the hull further.

**Physics.** Mass in KG **0.001** (true mass 0.86 g, and 1 g is a safer floor for the solver); check the derived
centre of mass against the pivot in-engine [36]. A 0.86 g rigid body is at the light end of what Chaos is happy
with — if it jitters on a surface, the fallback is to raise the override to 0.005 kg and note it in the listing,
not to make the tag thicker.

**Sockets.** Empties via `pipeline.make_socket`, restored after import from the `.sockets.json` sidecar because
Unreal 5.8.2 drops FBX sockets from any file containing a LodGroup, and remembering the socket scale-100 bug
[51][53]. Positions in the build frame (+X = top of the tag, +Z = out of the printed face):

| Socket | Position (mm) | Axes | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Face` | (0, 0, +0.075) | +Z out of the print | Place flat on a surface; decal and glow anchor |
| `Attach` | (0, 0, −0.075) | −Z out of the back | Stick to a wall, glue to a crate, parent to a kunai |
| `Fuse` | (+47.4, 0, +0.075) — the flame emblem centre | +Z, +X up the tag | Spark / burn VFX start; the scorch gradient in `_M.G` radiates from here |
| `Cord` | (+78.0, 0, 0) — top edge centre | +X out of the top | Ties to the kunai's `Ring` socket at (−103.5, 0, 0) mm [53]; also a fuse cord |

`Fuse` is at the emblem, not at the centre: a tag burns from the seal down. The `Cord` / `Ring` pairing is the
reason the kunai study reserved that socket, so the two assets should be dry-fitted together before release.

`"C:/Program Files/Blender Foundation/Blender 5.2/blender.exe" -b [file] --factory-startup --python script.py -- args`.
Never the MCP live link, never a GUI session. NumPy 2, so `np.ptp(a)` not `a.ptp()`. `PYTHONUTF8=1` for any
system-Python call. Every file that touches these strings is written UTF-8, and no raw kanji reaches a console.

**What the engine needs — the measured, non-negotiable list** [33][34][35][51][53]:

1. `UCX_<render mesh NODE name>_00`. Anything else silently lands with no collision.
2. Sockets are Empties (FBX Nulls) named `SOCKET_<node name>_<socket>`; a child *mesh* named `SOCKET_` is not a

3. **Import Mesh LODs ON.** The LodGroup carries no screen sizes, so apply 1.0 / 0.1710 / 0.0599 after import or

4. `T_PaperBomb_BC` imports **sRGB**; `_ORM` and `_M` import **linear** (sRGB off); `_N` as a normal map with

5. `T_PaperBomb_M` needs a mip chain: set **MipGenSettings = TMGS_FromTextureGroup**. A non-power-of-two PNG

6. Verify in a **second fresh process on the exact exported bytes** (SHA-256), under a new content path such as

   `WorkFiles/shuriken/UnrealShuriken/ShurikenValidation.uproject` — never `DemoGame_1`. One commandlet at a time,
   never left running; Git Bash rewrites `/Game` paths, so set `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1` or use PowerShell.
7. Nanite off: this is a 1,150-triangle prop with hand-authored LODs.

**The shuriken pack is frozen.** `Scripts/shuriken/**`, `Assets/Shuriken.blend`, `Exports/Shuriken/**` and

## 10. IP, and why this asset needs no "Created with AI" flag

### 10.1 Is the layout distinctive to a franchise?

| Element | Where it comes from | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A printed paper talisman with kanji and a red seal | Real: ofuda, kamifuda, senjafuda, gofu, 呪符 — centuries old, printed in black and vermilion on hōsho washi [1][3][6][7][13] | Public-domain folk culture |
| Clipped corners, ruled red frame, corner flourishes, seal boxes | Generic East Asian print and seal vocabulary | Generic |
| A paper talisman used as a weapon in fiction | A broad trope across many works, not one franchise [43] | Generic trope |
| **"Explosive tag" / 起爆札 / "paper bomb" as a named item, and a talisman centred on 爆** | **A Naruto-franchise invention** [42] | **Franchise-specific** |
| 火遁 | **Genuine historical term** — one of the 五遁の術 (wood, fire, earth, metal, water escapes), documented in the Edo-period *Bansenshūkai* [44][45][46] | Not franchise vocabulary |
| 焼尽 | Ordinary Japanese word, "burn up completely" | Generic |
| 爆炎陣, 瞬業, 火道 | Coined here and by the user from standard characters | Ours |

**Conclusion.** The tag's *form* is generic and safe. The single franchise-adjacent element is the large 爆 as the
centre of a talisman. A single common kanji is not protectable as such, our brush ring, flame emblem, border,
flourishes and seal marks are all our own drawing, and the franchise's own tag art is a plain rectangle with
radiating lines rather than an ornate ofuda — our design is visually unlike it. **Recommendation: keep the 爆**,
and control the risk where it actually lives, which is in the words on the listing.

**Standing pack rule, applied.** No franchise, brand or product name anywhere in an object, collection, material,
texture, socket, file, folder, report or listing string. `Scripts/pipeline/qa_check.py`'s `DENY_SUBSTRINGS` holds
only `naruto` (plus `kamish`, `gilgamesh`, `jinmuwon`) [56], and `Scripts/pipeline/**` is used as is — so
`props_lib` adds **its own** deny gate over: `naruto`, `kibaku`, `kibakufuda`, `uzumaki`, `konoha`, `shippuden`,
`boruto`, `akatsuki`, `hidden leaf`, `explosive tag`, `exploding tag`. Note that "paper bomb" itself is a
descriptive phrase and is used by many unrelated products; `PaperBomb` as the asset name is fine. "Ofuda",
"talisman" and "fuda" are safe generic words and are the better listing vocabulary.

**Geometry and art provenance.** Build from sections 4 and 5. Never trace fan-wiki images, replica photographs,
third-party 3D files, or the guide PNGs.

### 10.2 The guide images, and the AI flag

**What the guide images are.** `paperbomb_guide.png` was generated in ChatGPT. `paperbomb_guide_v2_real_glyphs.png`
is the user's edit of it with real kanji pasted in. The user's instruction was explicit: *"well you're going to
re-make it. the image is just our reference. So we are fine right"* and *"yes redraw the text with the brush
font."* So this is a **full original remake**.

**What actually ships.**

| Shipped artefact | How it is produced |
|---|---|
| `Assets/PaperBomb.blend` | Rebuilt from scratch by `Scripts/props/build_paper_bomb.py` |
| `Exports/PaperBomb/SM_PaperBomb.fbx` | Exported from that blend by `Scripts/pipeline/export_fbx.py` |
| `T_PaperBomb_BC / _ORM / _N / _M` | Baked from our own procedural node graph and our own curve geometry |
| Every character on the tag | Typeset by Blender from `MasaFont-Bold.ttf`, SIL OFL 1.1 [47] |
| Ring, rules, flourishes, flame emblem, seal marks, paper grain, wear | Drawn by our own brush and noise code |

**No pixel, curve, mask or shape is derived from the guide PNGs.** They are never opened by the build script; they
live only in `References/PaperBomb/`. Everything this study took out of them is the set of scalar numbers in
sections 3 and 5 — proportions and positions, which is the same class of information one takes from a photograph
or a tape measure.

**Fab's rule.** Epic made the AI question mandatory at publish time on 2025-05-22: creators must "select whether
their asset was created with a Generative AI Program," and "content that's not identified properly with the
'Created with AI' setting violates Fab terms" [38]. Fab's own support article states the obligation as applying to
"publishers who use AI to generate content for distribution" [39, SNIPPET — the page would not render for us].
Epic has published **no threshold** for AI used earlier in a workflow [38].

**Conclusion, which the user can rely on at listing time: this asset does not need the "Created with AI" flag.**
The reasoning, in one line: *nothing distributed in this product was generated by a generative AI program.* The
geometry is produced by our own deterministic code, the maps are baked from our own procedural material, and the
text is typeset from a licensed font. An AI-made picture was looked at to decide proportions, and looking at a
picture to decide how wide a border should be is an authoring decision, not generation — it produces none of the
shipped bytes.

**How to prove it, and keep proving it.**

1. The build script must **not read** `References/PaperBomb/*.png`. Gate it: fail the build if any path under
   `References/PaperBomb` is opened, and assert it in the build report.
2. Ship a hash manifest of every file in `Exports/PaperBomb/` in the build report, so the delivered bytes are
   traceable to a build.
3. Keep `WorkFiles/paperbomb/study_calc/*.json` as the audit trail: it is a measurement record, and it shows
   exactly what was and was not taken from the guides.

**Three honest caveats.** (a) This is our reading of Epic's published wording, not legal advice. (b) If the user
wants zero ambiguity they can tick the flag anyway — it costs discoverability with some buyers and nothing else —
or delete the guide PNGs once the art is signed off. (c) The fonts are SIL OFL 1.1, which lets us ship rendered
output freely; the `.ttf` files themselves must **not** be included in the Fab package, and MasaFont's Reserved
Font Name `KouzanBrushFontGyousyo` must not be used for any modified font file [47][48].

## 11. Open questions — ANSWERED

Every one of these was decided by building the thing and looking at it. The decision and the evidence are here;
the reasoning behind the original question is left above so the next asset can re-use it.

1. **Curl direction and depth.** ANSWERED: the printed face is CONCAVE (the sourced mechanism [25][26][27]) and
   the sagitta is **4.2 mm**, not 2.0. At 2.0 mm over 70 mm the card read as a flat plane in every gallery shot —
   the visual review called it a blocker, and it was right. 4.2 mm (R ≈ 146 mm) deepening to 6.0 mm over the last
   40 mm is a cup you can see from the end of the tag and still a sheet rather than a tube.
2. **Folds.** ANSWERED: **two**, at the thirds, and the dihedral is **7°** a crease over a 2.2 mm half-width, not
   the 175° dihedral this study quoted (which is 5°, and which the first build then softened to 3°). At under about
   5°/crease a fold throws no shading line and the AO bake finds nothing to occlude. See section 13.
3. **Torn edge.** ANSWERED: **geometry plus paint, on an OPAQUE material.** The torn outline is geometry at every
   LOD; the 0.4–0.8 mm fibre fringe is painted into the BASE COLOUR as a soft lightening (loose kozo fibre sits
   proud at a break and catches light), which works at every LOD and in every mip. `_M` red still carries the
   fringe alpha for a buyer who wants a Masked variant. The first build shipped the fringe only in `_M`, unwired,
   and the tear read as a die-cut wavy line.
4. **Pivot.** ANSWERED: **card mid-surface**, the pack's convention — but the question was asked of a nearly flat
   card and the shipped card spans about 12 mm of Z. "Lay it flat" is no longer a meaningful use for the Face
   socket, so Face is documented as a decal/glow/VFX anchor and the tag rests on its curl and its folded corner.
5. **Texel density.** ANSWERED: **accept 129 px/cm at 2048.** Measured 129.36 / 129.33 / 129.43 on the three LODs.
   4K stays reserved for hero and skin. Nothing in the review suggested the print reads soft.
6. **Back face.** ANSWERED: **faint mirrored show-through at 0.32**, drawn as TWO ghosts — sumi soaks into the
   sheet and vermilion sits on it, so each ink ghosts with its own weight and its own tint, blurred a full 0.9 mm.
   One ghost desaturated to a single sepia made the solid vermilion panel — the most opaque thing on the card —
   one of the faintest marks on the back, which is backwards.
7. **The 爆.** ANSWERED: **kept**, per section 10.1. The one-line `TEXT` config in `props_lib/paperbomb_art.py`
   still makes it a one-word swap if the user ever wants more distance.
8. **Physics mass.** DECIDED but NOT TESTED: ships with Mass-in-KG **0.001** and 0.005 documented as the fallback.
   No Chaos simulation has been run. The card must not be thickened to fix jitter.
9. **Variants.** STILL OPEN, and still cheap: `props_lib/sheet.py` now exists, and `_M` green already carries a
   burn-order field radiating from the Fuse socket, which is exactly what a scorched half-burnt tag needs.

## 13. What the build proved wrong

The first build followed this study closely and was reviewed three ways. These are the numbers that did not
survive contact with a render. They are corrected here because the folding fan and the smoke bomb will be built
from this document.

| section | this study said | shipped | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4, 6 | dihedral 175° (5° a crease), fold radius 0.5 mm | **7° a crease, 2.2 mm half-width** | Under ~5° a crease throws no shading line. 0.5 mm is a knife edge at this scale and forces the whole turn into a 1 mm band. |
| 4, 6 | curl sagitta 2.0 mm | **4.2 mm** (6.0 at the bottom) | At 2.0 mm the card reads as a decal plane in the hero shot. |
| 4, 6 | dog-ear folded back 25° | **105°** | 25° out of plane is invisible in all six gallery shots. 105° breaks the silhouette, throws a shadow and gives the AO bake something to find. |
| 4 | bounding box Z 2.6 mm | **12.15 mm** | Consequence of the three rows above. The bounds RADIUS is 85.518 mm against this study's 85.5, so the LOD screen sizes are unchanged. |
| — | (not in the study) | **the curl must relax at each crease** | A cupped sheet cannot be folded across its cup without strain: the metric picks up `1 − lift·dθ/dv`. Real paper pops the cup out at the crease. A SHORT relaxation ramp is no help — it trades one stretch term for a `d(lift)/dv` term exactly as large. |
| — | (not in the study) | **a smoothed fold must be a cylindrical bend** | Rotating a ray of fixed length through a ramping angle stretches by `sqrt(1 + (d·dφ/dd)²)` ≈ 1.37 at the middle of the band, whatever the soften distance. Roll the sheet round a radius `soften / Φ` instead. |
| 5.3 | upper columns at x 0.180 / 0.818, em 14.4 mm | **x 0.2214 / 0.7786, em 11.9 mm**, cells lowered 2.0 mm | The guide's flanking marks are invented scribbles and are narrow; MasaFont's real glyphs run about 1.3 em wide, so a 14.4 mm em draws 18.6 mm of ink. There is no centre on a 70 mm card at which that clears both the corner bracket and the flame emblem. |
| 5.1 | "a thin red double border" | outer rule + **four corner brackets**, inner rule on the top and bottom runs only | This study's own table has brackets, not a second rectangle. The two long side laps were what the kanji columns crossed and what stacked five near-parallel reds into the bottom corners. |
| 5.2 | centre em 67.1 mm, ink 50.4 × 62.4 (w/h 0.81) | **em 50 mm with a 1.12 vertical set**, ink ≈ 48 × 50 mm (w/h 0.92) | MasaFont's U+7206 sets 1.04 wide over tall; the guide's glyph is the opposite. A modest vertical set is the only way to get near the study's proportion without a 1.29 anamorphic. |
| 6 | sumi linear (0.035, 0.034, 0.032) | **(0.0480, 0.0470, 0.0445)** | The study's own value encodes to stored 0.202, under the study's own 60/255 = 0.235 dielectric floor. `DIELECTRIC_FLOOR_LINEAR` is now clamped per channel. Vermilion's G and B were lifted to the floor and its R left alone — a saturated pigment legitimately has channels below a band written for neutral dielectrics. |
| 6 | fibre grain on a 0.1–0.4 mm cell | **0.17 mm across the fibre, 0.70 mm along it**, high-passed | Value noise on a 0.26 mm cell peaks at about 0.52 mm and is isotropic, which reads as stucco. 0.17 mm is 2.2 texels at 12.923 px/mm — the finest a 2048 map can carry. The cell must also be ANISOTROPIC: kozo is 10–15 mm of long fibre lying down. |
| 7 | back island at u 0.458 | **u 0.4653** | At 0.458 the two rasters' padding overlaps by 15 px and each bake margin overwrites the other. |
| 8 | LOD2 "no dog-ear", band 100–200 | **dog-ear kept, band 100–240** | That content table was written when the fold was 25° and invisible. At 105° dropping it costs 6.2 mm of Hausdorff and a visible pop at the switch. |
| 8 | LOD0 tear refinement | **2.60 boundary points per mm** | At 0.95/mm the 16 mm torn stretch carried 14 points: at most 7 of the specified 6–12 lobes representable, 3–4 resolved, and the edge read as a sawtooth. |
| 3 (sockets) | Cord "+X out of the top" | (the first build shipped pitch −90) | The study was right and the build was wrong. The dry-fit against the kunai's Ring socket — which this study called for and the first build deferred — is the test that catches it, and it now runs on every build. |

Two measurement lessons worth carrying forward, because both produced a confident wrong number:

* **A LOD error measured one way is not a LOD error.** LODn → LOD0 is zero by construction for a vertex-subset
  LOD. Report the two-sided Hausdorff.
* **Tilt read off a normal map's BLUE channel is meaningless at 8 bits.** At a 3.4° slope `nz` encodes to 255, so
  blue says "perfectly flat" while red and green carry the whole signal. Measure the slope from red and green.
